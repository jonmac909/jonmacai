Rosa's House Hacks

In my family nothing was thrown away while it could still be fixed. My father kept a coffee tin of screws and a clamp that was older than I am, and between them he kept a whole house running. This book is what he knew, written down and checked, with the jobs nobody at home should touch said plainly.

I am not going to tell you that every repair is easy. I will tell you which ones are, in what order to do them, and when to put the tool down and call someone.

Rosa

Questions, answered plainly

I am not handy at all. Is it still for me?
Yes. Every fix starts with how to look at the problem, then lists what you need and the steps in order. Nothing assumes you have done it before. Chapter 1 teaches the five questions to ask before you touch anything, so even your first fix starts from the same careful place. If a step is unclear, stop and read the fix again from the top.
Does it tell me to buy a lot of tools?
No. The first chapters cover the small kit that handles most jobs, and the rest of the book uses it. A screwdriver, a clamp, glue, a plane and a needle cover most of the 60 fixes. Nothing in the kit is expensive, and the book says where to keep it so you can find it in a minute when something breaks. Most of the fixes use the same few items, so you buy them once.
What about jobs I should not touch?
Gas, mains wiring, structural work, sewage and large mold are called out plainly, with what to tell the professional when you call. The rule in the book is simple: if in doubt, it is a job for someone qualified, and you stop rather than push on.
Are the steps in plain English?
Yes. What to look at, what you need, the steps in order, and what can go wrong before you start. No brand names and no special tools. Every fix is written to be read once, start to finish, before you pick up a tool, and then followed step by step.
