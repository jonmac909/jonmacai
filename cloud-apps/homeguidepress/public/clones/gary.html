The gap that isn’t

“There is no shortage of drivers willing to work. There is a shortage of drivers willing to work for what is on offer.”

Drivers have said it for years, in cab chat, at truck stops and under every video about pay. The driving is rarely the problem. It is everything bolted on around it that eats the week.

  • Hours at the gateSat in a queue at a distribution centre, off the clock in all but name, while the booked slot comes and goes.
  • Rates that do not movePay that has barely shifted while diesel, food, rent and mortgages all went up.
  • Nowhere to parkNowhere safe to stop at the end of a shift, and nowhere decent to wash or eat when you do.
  • A cab that watches youTrackers, telematics scores and cameras, run by people who have never slept in a bunk.
  • The body sends the billLong days and early starts: the body takes them at twenty-five and pays for them at fifty.

If the gap is about willingness, a good driver has leverage. You just need three things.

What every hour really pays

Not the advertised rate. The real figure once every unpaid minute is counted.

What the rules actually say

Your hours, breaks, working time, pay for waiting, and the licence you are protecting.

What else is out there

Other firms, other work, agency, limited company or your own lorry, and what each one really pays.

Meet the author

Peter Collins

Peter is a UK HGV driver. On his YouTube channel, Peter Collins, he talks about the job as drivers actually live it: the pay, the hours, the waiting, the parking and the pressure, and the viewers who watch it are mostly drivers too.

These books grew out of the questions that keep coming up there. They set out how the money flows, what the rules really say, how to compare one job against another, and what a truck really costs to run, in plain English, from the driver’s side, and fair to the good employers and honest hauliers who do things properly.

Knowing your worth is never about cutting corners. A driver who knows the rules properly is harder to push around, not easier.

The books speak as books, not memoirs: examples are illustrations and their numbers are labelled as such. Where law, tax or health is involved, they tell you where to check: GOV.UK, DVSA, DVLA, HMRC, ACAS, an accountant or your GP.
