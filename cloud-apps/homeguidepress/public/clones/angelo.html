A letter from Giovanni

My mother never wrote a word of it down. So I did.

I was eight when my family came to Brooklyn. My mother Assunta took a small place on 18th Avenue in Bensonhurst, and I started at nine, peeling garlic on a milk crate by the back door, long before I was tall enough to see over the counter.

Thirty years later I am still at that stove. And in thirty years I never once saw her measure anything. How much salt the water takes, when the gravy has cooked long enough, which bread is stale enough for meatballs. It lived in her hands, and nowhere else.

I am not a chef, and I never pretend to be one. What I learned is simpler: the old way is almost always the easier way. She did less than you think, and she did it in the right order.

So I wrote it all down. Her dishes, the ones from my videos, and every small trick that makes them work. Learn the tricks, and the recipes become yours.

Giovanni

Eggplant, the old way

Salted, pressed, brushed, never fried. Serves 4 · 1 hour pressing · 25 minutes in the oven

You need

2 young eggplants, heavy for their size1 tbsp coarse salt 3 tbsp olive oil1 can (28 oz / 800 g) whole peeled tomatoes, for the weight ½ lemonBasil, a few leaves

Do this

  1. Slice into rounds as thick as your finger, about ½ in / 1.5 cm. Leave the skin on.
  2. Salt both sides, layer in a colander, and put a plate on top with the can of tomatoes on the plate. One hour.
  3. Oven to 425°F / 220°C with the empty tray inside. Wipe every slice dry with a towel.
  4. Brush the oil on the eggplant, not the tray. One layer on the hot tray, no slice touching.
  5. 20 to 25 minutes, turning once, until deep gold. Lemon and salt only at the end.

Giovanni's trick

The weight. Eggplant is a honeycomb full of air, and the air is what drinks the oil. Press the air out and a sponge stops being a sponge.

If it goes wrong

Pale and soft? The slices were crowded and steamed. Use two trays next time, and never cover them.
