I · The Backbone

Five rules worth more than any kit you could buy

The book has a lot of methods in it. Underneath all of them are five simple ideas. Forget everything else and keep these. With them you could work most of the book out yourself.

1

Work the outage backwards

Most people plan for the gap between the lights going out and the lights coming back. That is the wrong frame. Plan for the first hour, the first night and the first morning after, because that is where every real decision gets made.

2

Understand a thing before you buy it

Never buy a piece of kit until you understand the problem it solves. Half the money wasted on preparing goes on solving a problem you never had. Understanding is free, and it tells you when the cheap answer beats the expensive one.

3

Cheap and dull beats expensive and clever

Hot water bottles, a wool blanket, a torch, spare batteries, a bag of rice. The unglamorous things carry a household through a power cut far better than the shiny gadget. A plan that leans on one costly device working perfectly is a fragile plan.

4

Two cheap backups beat one clever solution

Two simple ways to boil water beat one big generator. Anything can fail, so never hang your safety on a single thing. Layer cheap, simple backups and you are never one breakdown away from trouble.

5

Safety first, every single time

No amount of comfort is worth carbon monoxide, a house fire, or bad water. The fastest way to turn an inconvenience into a tragedy is an unsafe shortcut for heat, light or cooking. When in doubt, take the safe option even if it is colder or slower.

Thirty years of UK weather, power cuts and cold snaps taught me how quickly ordinary life gets complicated when the lights go and the shops shut. It is rarely dramatic. Mostly it is quiet, and the households that do a few simple things in the right order come through fine.

So let me take one thing off your plate. You do not need a bunker, or a room full of gear with the labels still on.
