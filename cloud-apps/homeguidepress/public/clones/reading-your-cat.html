II.
The Habit

A small habit now. A clearer conversation with your vet later.

Most worries start as a small change: in eating, drinking, litter habits, sleep, voice or mood. A change can have many causes, some of them health related, and only a vet can tell which. The habit this book teaches is simple: know your cat's normal, notice the change early, write it down and call the vet first.

When a Cat Wants to Be Left Alone
From the book · Part 2

A bite from a cat that was purring a moment ago feels like betrayal. It is not. Most of the time the cat gave a series of small signals, the signals were missed, and the cat did the only thing left. Learning the early warnings protects you and keeps your cat's trust.

L
Straining and When It Is an Emergency
From the book · Part 4

Most litter box trouble is a slow puzzle. This chapter is different. Some changes at the litter box mean your cat needs a vet or an emergency clinic right now, not tomorrow, and not after another day of watching. Read this chapter once, calmly, so that you know what to do if the moment ever comes.

V
Call the Vet Today List
From the book · Part 9

Cats are good at hiding that they feel unwell. By the time you notice a change, it may have been building for a while. This chapter is a plan to read now, while everything is calm, so that on a bad day you do not have to think.

FAQ

Questions owners ask before they click.

Will this work for my cat?

It is written for ordinary household cats, indoor or in-and-out. There are whole parts for homes with more than one cat and for older cats, and every cat is a little different, so the book teaches you to learn your own cat's normal first.

Is this a replacement for my vet?

No, and it never pretends to be. The book helps you understand your cat and notice when something needs professional attention sooner. For any medical concern, diagnosis or emergency, always see a licensed veterinarian.

I'm a brand-new cat owner. Is it too advanced?

Not at all. It is written in plain words, start to finish, with no jargon. New owners get a calm foundation; longtime owners finally understand behaviors they have watched for years. Read it cover to cover or open straight to the part you need tonight.

Does it give medical advice or name medicines?

No. It is general cat care and behavior information. It names no medicines or doses and never diagnoses. Where a sign could be health related, it tells you to call your vet, and it lists the emergencies that need a vet or an emergency clinic at once.
