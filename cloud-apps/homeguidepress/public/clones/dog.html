Why is it so hard to know when your dog is not okay?

He's still eating. She's still wagging. Maybe he's a little slower on the stairs, or she's licking a paw more than usual. It never seems like much on its own.

But dogs are very good at hiding pain. By the time it's obvious, the problem has often been there for weeks. That's the hard part of loving a dog who can't tell you what hurts.

The problem isn't you. It's that nobody ever showed you what to look for.

Most of it is simpler than you think. A few minutes each week running your hands over your dog, nose to tail. Knowing which signs mean call the vet now, which mean call today, and which mean book a visit. Feeding the right amount of the right food. Keeping up with the little things, like teeth, nails and ears.

And something nice happens along the way. The same habits that help you catch trouble early also give you an easier dog. One that settles in, comes when called, and is calm when you leave. One you understand better, so you get more good years together.

Less guessing. Less worry. A happier, healthier dog. That's what this book is for.
