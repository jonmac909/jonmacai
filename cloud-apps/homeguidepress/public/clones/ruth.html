When the lights go out

Nine parts for the first hour, the first night and the first three days

Most outages are a small inconvenience that a little planning turns into a quiet few days at home. This book gives you the plan in plain words: what to do, in what order, and the safety rule that goes with it. Every chapter ends with something to do this week, and the printable cards go on the fridge or beside the bed.

The honest part

What this book is not

  • Not a scare book. There is no doom, no collapse and no fear selling. Outages are ordinary, usually short, and easier with a plan.
  • Not a gadget catalog. Things are described by type, such as a headlamp or a battery radio, with no brand names and no shopping spree. Most homes already have a lot of it.
  • Not medical or electrical advice. It is general household information. It tells you when to follow your appliance manuals, your utility and local authority, and when to ask a doctor, pharmacist or equipment supplier.

Eight printables, included

The Power-Out Card, Household Needs Sheet, Food Safety Clock, Carbon Monoxide Rules, Water Plan, Three-Day Kit List, Contact Card and 72-Hour Checklist, at the end of the book. Print them on plain paper and put them by the front door, on the fridge and beside the bed.
